1 · The setting

A country built on a collision

The Philippines sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, trapped between two big plates pushing toward each other. Oceanic plates sink (subduct) under the islands at trenches on both sides. The biggest and most active is the Philippine Trench in the east. Tap any line, dot or volcano.

3 · Susceptibility

Why the Philippines shakes so much

Squeezed from both sides

Subduction zones on opposite sides face each other: the Philippine Trench in the east and the Manila, Negros, Sulu and Cotabato trenches in the west. The islands are crushed in between, so the crust is broken by many faults.

Fast-moving plates

The Philippine Sea Plate moves several centimeters a year toward the islands. Every year of movement adds stress that is released in earthquakes.

Tsunamis from the trenches

Subduction quakes happen under the sea and can lift the seafloor. The 1976 Moro Gulf tsunami killed thousands of people in minutes.

People live on the faults

Over 110 million Filipinos live on the coasts or near active faults. The West Valley Fault runs right under Metro Manila.

Ranked #1 for disaster risk

The WorldRiskReport ranked the Philippines the most at-risk country in the world to natural hazards in 2022, 2023 and 2024.

Earthquakes + volcanoes

The same subduction that causes the quakes also melts the mantle and feeds volcanoes like Mayon, Taal, Pinatubo and Kanlaon.

4 · Magnitude

How much stronger is a bigger quake?

Each step up in magnitude releases about 32 times more energy. Two steps is about 1,000 times. Slide or tap a level to see what it does.

Releases about × the energy of an M4.0, roughly tonnes of TNT.

    Magnitude is the energy released at the source. How hard the ground shakes where you are (intensity) also depends on distance and depth.

    5 · Be ready

    When the ground shakes

    1

    DROP

    Get down on your hands and knees before the shaking knocks you down.

    2

    COVER

    Get under a sturdy table. Protect your head and neck from falling objects.

    3

    HOLD ON

    Hold on to the table until the shaking stops. Then evacuate calmly.

    Near the coast? Strong shaking or the sea suddenly pulling back are natural tsunami warnings. Go to high ground right away. Don't wait for an alert.
